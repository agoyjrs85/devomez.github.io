Political consultant Jim Middleton told Sky News the resistance to Scott Morrison’s slow move toward net zero emissions is a reminder it is an issue for both sides of politics and not just Labor.

“It’s been my observation that for some months … Scott Morrison’s been moving slowly ... not quite imperceptibly away from coal, through gas, to renewables and hoping those recalcitrants, particularly within the National Party wouldn’t notice what’s going on,” he said.

“Well they have … now. Matt Canavan and Bridget McKenzie and Barnaby Joyce, all former ministers, this is quite significant that they’re standing up to the prime minister.

“In Matt Canavan’s case even acknowledging that he would be prepared to cross the floor to represent the interests of his constituents".

Mr Middleton said the resistance not only puts pressure on Scott Morrison but also Nationals Leader Michael McCormack who does not enjoy the broad support previous Nationals leaders have had.

“It is a problem, it is a difficulty and it reminds us that this is an issue for both sides of politics, not just for Labor who have made heavy weather of it for the past … months,” he said.
