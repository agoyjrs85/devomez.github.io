The legal defense of Entrapment is often misunderstood. Criminal defense lawyer Ryan Pacyga discusses the basics of the law of entrapment. Generally, the defense applies when law enforcement and/or their informants attempt to coerce someone into committing a crime that the person would not otherwise commit. The coercion needs to be fairly strong, and once you make the initial showing of coercion (by badgering, persuasion, etc) then the prosecution can try to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that you were predisposed to commit the crime anyway. There is another type of entrapment, called "due process entrapment" that deals with extreme police conduct that shocks the conscience. Finally, there is sentencing entrapment/manipulation that I will discuss in a different video. I have successfully defended cases involving entrapment. Each case is unique.
